The problem

Reading a script still means looking away.

Every teleprompter today assumes a screen you stare at. On stage, on camera, or on a sales call, that means broken eye contact, a frozen "reading" voice, and notes you can't glance at without it being obvious. The hardware to fix this finally exists. The software didn't — until Spector.

The product

Not just another teleprompter.

Pacing

Auto-paced, punctuation-aware

Advances at speaking pace on its own — lingering on sentence ends, breathing at commas, holding on emphasis. No manual scroll.

Comfort

Spatial anchoring

Kalman-filtered head-tilt stabilizes text to reduce eye strain. Breathing animations create natural, immersive flow.

Analytics

Post-run intelligence

Avg WPM, pacing consistency, hesitation detection, and slowest-moment analysis after every rehearsal run.

Comfort mode

Text that holds still while you don't.

Spatially anchored. Calm by design.

Standard teleprompters shake with every head movement — causing eye strain and that frozen "reading" look. Comfort mode locks your script in space so it stays stable and readable through an entire rehearsal.

FAQ

The honest answers.

Which devices actually work today?

Any phone or desktop browser works right now — adaptive pacing, Comfort mode (using your device's real orientation sensors), and all controls. On smart glasses that display web content (like Ray-Ban Meta Display), the HUD runs and Comfort's spatial effects use the glasses' real sensors. Right temple-button advance is built and ready; validating it on real hardware is exactly what our current beta is for.

Hasn't Meta already built a teleprompter?

It has — Meta added a basic teleprompter to its $799 Ray-Ban Display glasses in 2026. But it's locked to Meta's hardware, US-only, account-required, and advanced by hand. That's exactly the point: platform owners build for their own device. Spector is cross-brand and neutral — rehearse on your phone today, then perform on-lens today on XREAL or Viture, with Ray-Ban Meta and others coming as their platforms open up (see the honest device matrix) — and open source, so it moves faster and can't be locked down.

Is my script and rehearsal data private?

Free core, forever. No account, no cloud, nothing uploaded — ever. Your scripts and rehearsal history are stored locally in your own browser and never leave it. Because the whole app is open source, you can verify exactly what it does. The only time you share anything is if you choose to email us or join the beta.

What does it cost?

The core teleprompter — on phone and glasses — is free, today, and the open-source core stays free. Down the line, a paid tier may add power-user extras like deeper rehearsal analytics and sync. Nothing's behind a paywall right now, and founding beta testers get any future Pro free for life (see below). Full breakdown on the Pricing page.

How do I become a beta tester?

No glasses needed to start — try the full rehearsal experience in your browser right now. If you own Ray-Ban Meta, XREAL, Viture, or Brilliant Labs hardware, join the hardware beta to help validate temple controls and the HUD on real devices. Founding testers get Spector Pro free for life and a credit.

Is it really open source?

Yes — the project is public on GitHub. Read the code, file issues, or build on it. Questions or ideas? Use GitHub Discussions.
