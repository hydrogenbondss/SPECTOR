FAQ

The honest answers.

Which devices actually work today?

Any phone or desktop browser works now, including adaptive pacing, Comfort mode, and the full control set. XREAL and Viture work as display glasses through device mirroring. Ray-Ban Meta models without a display are useful for rehearsal only, and Ray-Ban Meta Display support depends on Meta opening its platform. The device matrix is the source of truth.

Hasn't Meta already built a teleprompter?

Meta added a basic teleprompter to its $799 Ray-Ban Display glasses in 2026. It is tied to Meta hardware and platform access. Spector takes a cross-brand approach: rehearse on any browser, use XREAL or Viture as display glasses today, and add other hardware as those platforms permit. The project is open source, so compatibility work is visible.

Is my script and rehearsal data private?

The core is free. No account or cloud storage is required. Scripts and rehearsal history stay in your browser. Because the app is open source, you can inspect how that storage works. Information leaves your device only when you choose to email us or submit the hardware beta form.

What does it cost?

The complete product available today is free. A future optional Pro tier may add services such as cross-device sync and extended history, but it is not for sale and no final price has been announced. See the pricing page for the current plan.

How do I become a beta tester?

No glasses are needed to start. Try the full rehearsal experience in your browser. If you own Ray-Ban Meta, XREAL, Viture, or Brilliant Labs hardware, join the hardware beta to help validate temple controls and the HUD on real devices.

Is it really open source?

Yes. The project is public on GitHub. Read the code, file issues, or build on it. Questions and ideas belong in GitHub Discussions.
